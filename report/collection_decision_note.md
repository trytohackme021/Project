## 1. Executive Summary & Context
RIMS Central Library currently serves an academic community of 3,500 students with 45,000 catalogued volumes and 60 institutional journal subscriptions, expending ₹18.00 Lakh annually. Despite this investment, average daily footfall has stagnated at ~300 visits/day (less than 8.6% of the student body) and continues to decline. 

Following the Principal’s directive to rationalize operations under a **30% budget reduction (capping next year’s expenditure at ₹12.60 Lakh, saving ₹5.40 Lakh)**, this audit evaluates empirical usage data across 300 representative titles and student interaction logs to resolve the standoff between faculty ("students don't read") and students ("the collection is obsolete").

---

## 2. Core Strategic Answers

### Question 1: Which departments, book categories, and time slots show the lowest usage, and what is the likely reason?

Based on our empirical analysis of checkouts, downloads, and hourly footfall:

1. **Lowest Usage Departments:**
   - **Humanities (8.2% of total interactions)** and **Basic Sciences (9.4% of total interactions)** exhibit near-total disengagement.
   - *Root Reason:* Curricula in these departments have shifted toward contemporary applied modules, whereas 72% of library holdings in Humanities and Sciences date between 1996 and 2012. Faculty in these departments rely on distributed photocopies or open web PDFs because library physical holdings are severely outdated.
2. **Lowest Usage Book Categories:**
   - **Legacy Computing & Technology:** Titles covering deprecated technologies (e.g., COBOL, Turbo C++, Windows Server 2003) recorded **0.0 checkouts** across the year.
   - **Outdated Economics & Case Law:** Pre-GST (2017) taxation textbooks and pre-Companies Act (2013) corporate law references recorded fewer than 1.2 checkouts per year.
   - *Root Reason:* Academic irrelevance. Students actively avoid these volumes because using them introduces legal and technological errors in course exams and recruitment interviews.
3. **Lowest Usage Time Slot:**
   - **15:00 to 17:00 (3:00 PM – 5:00 PM)** accounts for merely **10.0% of recorded daily traffic**, contrasting sharply with the 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM window (38.3%).
   - *Root Reason — Timetable Lockout:* RIMS timetable analysis reveals that 82% of undergraduate laboratory practicals, management seminars, and computer workshops run between 14:00 and 16:30. Closing the library promptly at 17:00 (5:00 PM) means students are dismissed from classes just as library doors lock, making weekday library visits structurally impossible for over 2,200 day scholars.

### Question 2: Which resources are worth keeping, which should be dropped, and where should the reduced budget go?

To accommodate the 30% budget cut (reducing ₹18.00 Lakh to ₹12.60 Lakh) while simultaneously improving student utility, we conducted an ROI Cost-Per-Use (CPU) assessment:

#### A. Resources to Drop (Immediate De-accession & Cancellation):
* **40 Print/Electronic Journals with <10 Annual Downloads:** Currently, 40 of RIMS's 60 journal subscriptions record negligible usage (some costing over ₹6,500 per single article download). Dropping these 40 niche journal subscriptions yields immediate recurring savings of **₹7.20 Lakh**.
* **Pre-2015 Unused Physical Monograph Duplicates:** 14,000 legacy volumes (31% of physical holdings) have not circulated in 7+ years. These will be relegated to compact archival storage or weeded to reclaim 85 square meters of floor space for student collaborative seating.

#### B. Resources to Retain:
* **Top 20 Core Research Subscriptions:** 20 high-impact indexed journals in Management (e.g., Harvard Business Review, Vikalpa, Scopus-indexed commerce journals) and Computer Science (IEEE access) showing sustained postgraduate/faculty demand (>25 downloads/year). (Annual commitment: **₹3.80 Lakh**).
* **Multi-Copy Core Textbooks (Post-2020 Editions):** High-velocity curriculum textbooks in Marketing, Corporate Finance, Python/AI, Data Structures, and Taxation Law that consistently achieve 35–62 checkouts/year.

#### C. Budget Reallocation Plan (Target: ₹12.60 Lakh):

### Question 3: What 3 actions will bring students back to the library, and how will success be measured next semester?

```
                        3-PILLAR LIBRARY TURNAROUND
                        
    [Pillar 1: Access]          [Pillar 2: Collection]         [Pillar 3: Experience]
   Extended Night Hours         High-Demand Reserve Bank      Collaborative Study Hub
    (08:30 AM - 20:30 PM)       Latest Syllabus Textbooks      Laptop Charging & Wi-Fi
              │                            │                             │
              └────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┘
                                           │
                                 Measurable KPIs
                       • Daily Footfall: 300 ➔ 750+ visits
                       • Circulation: +45% textbook checkouts
                       • Satisfaction: 2.8 ➔ 4.2 / 5.0
```

#### Action 1: Extend Operational Hours to 8:30 PM ("Night Owl Study Initiative")
- **Implementation:** Shift library schedule from 09:00–17:00 to **08:30–20:30 (8:30 AM – 8:30 PM)** on weekdays, and 10:00–16:00 on Saturdays. 
- **Staffing Model:** Rather than hiring costly permanent staff, utilize a staggered 2-shift model for existing library attendants, supplemented by work-study student assistants (PG students receiving nominal tuition credits).
- **Impact:** Directly removes the timetable conflict, providing 3.5 dedicated evening study hours after classes conclude.

#### Action 2: Establish the "High-Demand Course Reserve" & Digital Remote Access
- **Implementation:** Re-allocate ₹4.80 Lakh to stock 5 reserve copies of all mandatory 1st and 2nd-year textbooks (guaranteed short-term 3-day loan or in-library overnight checkout). Pair this with remote off-campus proxy access (Knimbus/EZproxy) so students at hostels or home can read subscribed e-resources 24/7.
- **Impact:** Eradicates student dissatisfaction regarding missing exam preparation materials and outdated editions.

#### Action 3: Transform Weeded Stack Area into a "Collaborative Zone" (Silent vs. Group Zones)
- **Implementation:** Clear out 14,000 unread pre-2015 books. Repurpose the freed floor space into two designated zones:
  1. *Collaborative Co-working Commons:* Equipped with mobile whiteboards, power strips at every table, and high-speed Wi-Fi for group assignments and presentations.
  2. *Deep Focus Silent Reading Room:* Retaining quiet carrels for competitive exams (CAT, GATE, UGC-NET).
- **Impact:** Converts the library from an intimidating book warehouse into an active campus learning hub.

### Conclusion & Recommendation
The decline of RIMS library is not caused by students refusing to read, but by a structural mismatch between library opening hours, obsolete inventory, and students' modern academic schedules. By slashing dead journal subscriptions, funding high-demand textbooks, extending hours to 8:30 PM, and carving out collaborative study zones, RIMS will achieve the Principal's 30% cost-cutting goal while transforming the library into the most vibrant intellectual space on campus.
