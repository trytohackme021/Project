# Central Library Usage & Collection Analytics (Julius-Style Analysis Report)
**Institution:** Rajasthan Institute of Management & Sciences (RIMS)  
**Author:** Library Strategic Consultancy Task Force  
**Target Audience:** Principal & Library Steering Committee  
**Dataset Reference:** `rims_library_data.csv` (300 audited titles & interaction records)

---

## Executive Overview
This analysis audits the core drivers behind the decline in daily visits (from ~300 visits/day downward) and identifies budgetary inefficiencies across RIMS's ₹18,00,000 annual library budget.

**Metrics:** Total physical checkouts (issues) vs. digital downloads segmented across 5 departments.

> **Insight:** *Management and Computer Science account for 68% of all library interactions, with CS exhibiting high digital download volume (1,280+ downloads). *Key Data Points:*
- Management: 1,340 physical checkouts, 480 digital downloads.
- Computer Science: 920 physical checkouts, 1,284 digital downloads.
- Basic Sciences & Humanities: Combined total represents less than 14% of overall campus engagement.

## Graph 2: Physical Book Checkouts vs. Publication Year
**File Link:** [graph_2_issues_vs_publication_year.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/project_1_empty_library/analysis/graph_2_issues_vs_publication_year.png)  
**Metrics:** Times issued last year plotted against the publication/edition year of the holding.

### 1-2 Lines of Key Insight:
> **Insight:** *There is an unmistakable "currency cliff" where books published prior to 2015 average fewer than 2.1 checkouts per year, confirming students' grievance that the collection is severely outdated. In contrast, textbooks updated to 2020–2024 editions circulate heavily (30 to 60+ checkouts), proving high demand when resources match modern syllabi.*

*Key Data Points:*
- 45% of surveyed catalog books are older than 10 years (pre-2015).
- Zero checkouts were recorded for 67 legacy titles (e.g., outdated COBOL manuals and 1990s management casebooks).
- 2021–2024 syllabus-aligned textbooks experience frequent waitlists (up to 62 issues/year per copy).

## Graph 3: Annual Cost vs. Annual Usage (Resource Efficiency Quad)
**File Link:** [graph_3_cost_vs_usage.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/project_1_empty_library/analysis/graph_3_cost_vs_usage.png)  
**Metrics:** Total annual interaction volume (Issues + Downloads) plotted against individual annual holding cost (₹).

### 1-2 Lines of Key Insight:
> **Insight:** *Subscription journals represent the single largest financial drain, consuming over ₹33.9 lakh (extrapolated across 60 journals) while 60.6% register fewer than 10 downloads per year (some costing ₹6,500+ per download). High-utility textbooks and multi-user digital databases provide 12x higher cost efficiency (averaging ₹25–₹80 per use).*

*Key Data Points:*
- 40 out of 66 sample journals had fewer than 10 annual downloads.
- Average cost of an underutilized journal: ₹51,400.
- E-resources bundles deliver between 50 and 180 downloads at an average cost of ₹28,000, offering the optimal return on investment.

## Graph 4: Student Visit Distribution by Operating Time Slot
**File Link:** [graph_4_visits_by_time_slot.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/project_1_empty_library/analysis/graph_4_visits_by_time_slot.png)  
**Metrics:** Proportion and raw count of library visits across standard operating hours (09:00 to 17:00).

### 1-2 Lines of Key Insight:
> **Insight:** *Peak library footfall occurs during the 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM lunch window (38.3%), followed by an aggressive 60% collapse between 3:00 PM and 5:00 PM (10.0%). Closing at 5:00 PM locks out students whose lecture and laboratory timetables run until 4:30 PM, artificially suppressing evening footfall.*

*Key Data Points:*
- 09:00–11:00 AM: 96 visits (32.0%)
- 11:00–13:00 PM: 115 visits (38.3%) [Peak]
- 13:00–15:00 PM: 59 visits (19.7%)
- 15:00–17:00 PM: 30 visits (10.0%) [Squeeze before early closure]

## Graph 5: Student Satisfaction Rating by Academic Cohort
**File Link:** [graph_5_satisfaction_by_year.png](file:///C:/Users/Asus/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/project_1_empty_library/analysis/graph_5_satisfaction_by_year.png)  
**Metrics:** 5-point Likert satisfaction scores compared across UG-1st Year to PG-2nd Year cohorts.

### 1-2 Lines of Key Insight:
> **Insight:** *Student satisfaction declines progressively from UG-1st Year (mean: 3.65/5.0) down to PG-2nd Year (mean: 2.15/5.0), reflecting that while junior students can manage with basic general textbooks, senior and postgraduate students find the library's research tools and advanced monographs completely deficient.*

*Key Data Points:*
- UG-1st Year: Mean satisfaction = 3.65 (Content with quiet study hall and introductory books).
- UG-2nd & 3rd Year: Mean satisfaction = 2.95 (Frustrated by lack of textbook copies).
- PG-1st & 2nd Year: Mean satisfaction = 2.15 – 2.24 (Stymied by restricted off-campus journal access and obsolete research material).
