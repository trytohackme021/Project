### Slide 3: Empirical Audit: Departmental Disparity & Outdated Holdings
**Slide Title:** Finding #1: The Currency Cliff & Syllabus Disconnect  
**Key Data Findings:**
- **Departmental Imbalance:** Management & Computer Science account for 68% of all library interactions. Basic Sciences & Humanities make up under 14%.
- **The "Pre-2015" Currency Cliff:** 45% of physical books in the audit were published before 2015. These older books average fewer than 2.1 checkouts per year (67 titles had 0 checkouts).
- **High Demand for Modern Titles:** Books published between 2021 and 2024 circulated 35 to 62 times per year, with persistent waitlists.
- **Conclusion:** Students *do* read—when the library stocks books that match their active exam syllabus and placement requirements.
- **Speaker Notes:** "Look at Graph 2 in our report. Checkouts fall off a cliff for pre-2015 books. Students aren't avoiding reading; they are avoiding obsolete 2004 textbooks that will cause them to fail modern university exams."

---

### Slide 4: Financial Drain: The Journal ROI Deficit
**Slide Title:** Finding #2: High-Cost Journals with Single-Digit Downloads  
**Financial Reality Check:**
- **60 Subscriptions consume ₹11.00 Lakh** (Over 61% of total library procurement funds).
- **60.6% Dormancy Rate:** 40 out of 66 audited journals logged **fewer than 10 downloads** all year.
- **Extreme Cost Per Use (CPU):** Several niche foreign journals cost between **₹4,500 and ₹7,800 per download**.
- **The Digital Opportunity:** Multi-user e-resource packages (e-books/curated databases) averaged 110+ downloads at a cost of only ₹180 to ₹320 per download.
- **Strategic Decision:** Cancel dormant individual subscriptions; transition to consortium packages.
- **Speaker Notes:** "Our biggest budget leak is not textbooks—it is legacy journal subscriptions. ₹11 Lakh goes to journals that average under 8 downloads a year. By cutting these 40 titles, we save ₹7.20 Lakh in one stroke."

### Slide 5: The Timetable Lockout: Why Students Can't Visit
**Slide Title:** Finding #3: The 5:00 PM Operating Hours Paradox  
**Footfall Timeline Breakdown:**
- **09:00 – 11:00 AM:** 32.0% of visits (Morning gap between lectures)
- **11:00 – 13:00 PM (1:00 PM):** 38.3% of visits (Lunch-hour peak rush)
- **13:00 – 15:00 PM (3:00 PM):** 19.7% of visits
- **15:00 – 17:00 PM (5:00 PM):** **10.0% of visits (60% footfall collapse)**
**The Root Cause:**
- 82% of student lab sessions, commerce practicals, and MBA presentations run until 4:30 PM.
- Locking library doors at 5:00 PM effectively shuts out 2,200+ day scholars.
- **Speaker Notes:** "Why does footfall crater after 3 PM? Because our timetable schedules practicals until 4:30 PM. Closing at 5 PM makes the library inaccessible precisely when students finally have free study time."

### Slide 7: The 3-Pillar Turnaround Plan
**Slide Title:** Three High-Impact Actions to Revitalize the Library  
**Pillar 1: Night Owl Hours (08:30 AM to 08:30 PM)**
- Staggered attendant shifts + work-study PG student monitors.
- Unlocks 3.5 dedicated evening study hours after lectures conclude.
**Pillar 2: Guaranteed Course Reserve Bank**
- Dedicated Reserve desk holding 5 fresh copies of every required syllabus textbook.
- Short-term overnight checkouts to eliminate exam panic.
**Pillar 3: The Collaborative Learning Commons**
- Weed 14,000 dead volumes to liberate 85 sq. meters.
- Split space into: (A) Silent Deep Focus Zone & (B) Collaborative Discussion Zone with whiteboards and laptop power sockets.
- **Speaker Notes:** "These three pillars remove the physical and structural barriers: students can now enter after classes, find the exact books they need, and study in groups with their laptops."
